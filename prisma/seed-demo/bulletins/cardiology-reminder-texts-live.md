Cardiology recheck reminders now go out by text, two days before the appointment. The change is small on paper and noticeable in practice: the recheck population is the one that misses appointments most often, and a text reaches them where a letter and a voicemail have both failed this year.

## What is different

- **The client gets a text 48 hours ahead**, with the date, time, and a one-tap way to reschedule.
- **The CSR desk is copied on every reminder**, so a reschedule that comes back is visible the moment it arrives.
- **The clinician's recheck interval is unchanged.** The text is a reminder, not a rebooking engine — if the clinician wants a patient back sooner, that conversation still happens in the room.

## For the desk

If a client replies to a reminder asking a clinical question, route it to the cardiology team rather than answering it at the desk. The simplest way is to log it against the patient and tag cardiology; they see the replies in the same place they see the schedule.

> [!tip] Reminders are the cheapest appointment we have. If you notice a recheck population where the text is not landing — a client who says they never received one — tell the cardiology lead. It is worth chasing, because the fix is usually one wrong digit.
