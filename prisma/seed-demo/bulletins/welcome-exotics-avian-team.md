This week the Exotics & Avian service stopped being a one-person operation. That is a bigger change than it sounds — for two years, every reptile, bird, and small mammal that came through the emergency door waited on a single clinician's schedule, and the honest answer to "can you see my rabbit today?" was often no.

That answer is now yes.

:::spotlight
- **YB** Dr. Yara Benitez — Exotics & Avian Specialist
- **FN** Forest Nakamura — Exotics Technician
:::

## What has changed for the rest of the hospital

- **Exotics consults can be booked the same day** during opening hours, not only through the emergency door.
- **The exotics ward now holds four enclosures** with independent temperature control, so an amphibian and a parrot are no longer sharing a room's climate.
- **Any ER patient that is not a cat or a dog** can be handed to the exotics service for the clinical parts of its stay, with the ER keeping ownership of the emergency plan.

> [!tip] If you are unsure whether a patient counts as "exotic", the answer is almost always yes if you had to look up how to handle it. Call the service — nobody has ever been teased for asking.

Please say hello when you see them on the floor. Both have been thrown into a busy autumn, and a friendly face on a long shift is worth more than a welcome email.
