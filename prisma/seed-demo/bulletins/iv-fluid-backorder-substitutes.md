Our primary supplier has placed our standard crystalloid on allocation through the first week of November. We have enough stock to run normally for roughly ten days; after that, the substitutions in the attached list keep the service running without a clinical compromise.

## How this works in practice

- **Do not ration fluid therapy.** The clinical decision is the clinician's and is unchanged. This notice is about *which bag* comes off the shelf, not whether a patient gets one.
- **Check the attached list before you draw up.** It maps each of our usual products to the approved alternative and the situations the alternative is not suitable for.
- **Log substitutions in the fluid register** against the patient, so pharmacy can see where the shortage is landing.

> [!warning] Substitutions are for the standard crystalloid range only. Colloids, blood products, and anything the clinician has specified by name are unaffected and are *not* to be swapped without the clinician's explicit agreement.

## What we are doing

Pharmacy has placed standing orders with two secondary suppliers and is monitoring daily. If the allocation lifts early, this notice will be retired early; if it extends, you will get a further notice rather than an amended one, so that the pinned timeline in the board stays honest.

The attached list is the authority — treat anything not on it as unavailable until pharmacy confirms otherwise.
