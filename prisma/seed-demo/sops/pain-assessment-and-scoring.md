**Owner:** Pain Management & Rehabilitation · **Review cycle:** Every 12 months · **Applies to:** Every staff member who handles a patient in hospital

## Purpose

Pain is a clinical parameter, recorded like temperature and heart rate, and it belongs in the record on every set of rounds — not only when an animal looks uncomfortable. A patient that is quiet on a painful injury is not necessarily comfortable; it may simply have stopped complaining.

## Scoring

Use the service's composite scale, scoring in the patient's own normal posture. Each domain is scored independently, and the total drives the intervention, not any single number.

| Domain | What to observe | Score range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Behaviour | Posture, willingness to move, response to the handler | 0–3 |
| Interactive | Response to gentle touch near, but not on, the injured area | 0–3 |
| Body tension | Abdominal or limb guarding when handled | 0–3 |
| Vocalisation | Crying, groaning, or silence that is out of character | 0–3 |

> [!tip] Score the patient *before* you move them for the exam, then again after analgesia. Pain that does not change with treatment is either not pain or not adequately treated, and both are findings worth recording.

## When to score

- On admission, then at least once every shift for every in-patient.
- Before and after any intervention that could reasonably change comfort.
- Any time a handler notices a change, regardless of when the last score was taken.
- Immediately before a procedure that requires handling of a painful area.

## Escalation

A rising score is acted on by the veterinarian on the case, and the response is recorded against the score that prompted it. **==Never leave a rising pain score without an entry against it.==** An unexplained rise is the finding a case review looks for first.

> [!note] Analgesia is ordered by the attending veterinarian. This procedure covers assessment and the language used to escalate; it does not contain drug choices or doses, and none should be inferred from it.

## Reassessment

Reassessment happens at a time set by the veterinarian at the moment of the order — "reassess in twenty minutes" — and the reassessment is charted even when the patient looks better, so the record shows *why* comfort improved rather than only that it did.

## Documentation

- The score is a number per domain, with a one-line note on what changed since the last score.
- A score taken by a technician and not reviewed by a clinician by the end of the shift is flagged at handover.
- Scores are entered into the shared trend view so the same patient is compared against itself, never against another patient.
