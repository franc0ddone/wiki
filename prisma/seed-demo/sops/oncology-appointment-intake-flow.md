**Owner:** Medical Oncology · **Review cycle:** Every 12 months · **Applies to:** Oncology staff and client services

## Purpose

Set up an oncology visit so the clinician spends the appointment on the patient rather than on missing information. The intake flow exists to have the day's plan, the previous treatment record, and the client's expectations all in front of the clinician before the patient is on the table.

## Before the visit

| When | Who | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 48 hours ahead | CSR | Confirm the visit and ask whether the client will need an estimate before treatment |
| 24 hours ahead | Oncology technician | Pull the treatment history and last lab work into the chart |
| Morning of | Oncology clinician | Review the plan and flag any decision the client must be part of |

> [!note] A client who has not been told whether treatment will happen at this visit should be called, not surprised. The most common cause of an unhappy oncology visit is a plan the client was never part of.

## On arrival

:::steps
1. **Check the client in and flag the estimate need** — so the clinician knows before the exam whether authorisation must be obtained.
2. **Weigh the patient** — on the same scale every visit, and record it before any treatment decision is discussed.
3. **Take a history at the door** — appetite, energy, and any new symptom since the last visit, in the client's own words.
4. **Place the patient comfortably** — with the visit's expected duration in mind, and with the client told what happens next.
5. **Hand the chart to the clinician** — with the treatment history, the weight trend, and the flagged decisions on the top sheet.
:::

## During and after

- The clinician explains the day's plan to the client in the room, and the client's questions are answered before treatment starts.
- Anything that will cost more than the estimate is authorised by the client before it happens.
- The next visit is booked before the client leaves, and the client is told what the hospital will ask them to watch for in between.

> [!tip] Book the next appointment in the room, on the client's calendar if they keep one. An oncology client leaving without a date carries a small uncertainty through the whole interval, and it costs more to resolve by phone than in person.
