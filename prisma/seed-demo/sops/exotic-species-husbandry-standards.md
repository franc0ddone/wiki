**Owner:** Exotics & Avian Service · **Review cycle:** Every 12 months · **Applies to:** Anyone who houses, feeds, or handles a non-traditional pet patient

## Purpose

A reptile, bird, or small mammal that is cold, dehydrated, or ventilated badly will not respond to treatment no matter how good the medicine is. Husbandry *is* treatment on this service, and it is the one part of the stay a technician can improve without waiting for a veterinarian.

## Species groups and their baseline needs

Housing is chosen from the patient's group, then adjusted to the individual animal. When a species does not appear here, the on-call exotics clinician sets the parameters in writing before the patient is housed.

| Group | Temperature | Humidity | Housing note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reptiles | Graded gradient, warm end to cool end | 50–70 %, species dependent | Always provide a hiding place and a temperature gradient, never one uniform heat |
| Birds | 24–28 °C for most companion species | 40–60 % | Perch off the floor; cover the cage front to reduce visual stress |
| Small mammals | 20–24 °C, draught-free | 30–50 % | Bedding deep enough to burrow; no cedar or pine shavings |
| Amphibians | Cool, 18–22 °C for most | High, species dependent | Water quality is the treatment; change with conditioned water only |

> [!note] Room air is a treatment parameter for this service. An incubator set by guesswork is worse than no incubator, because it hides a problem the animal cannot voice.

## Admission

:::steps
1. **Identify the species before it is housed** — a "lizard" is not enough. Confirm species, and confirm it with the owner in writing.
2. **Record the home environment** — temperature, substrate, diet, and water source as the owner keeps them, not as they should be.
3. **Set the hospital environment deliberately** — and write the set points on the cage card so the next shift does not adjust them by feel.
4. **Weigh on admission** — a gram-scale weight is the most sensitive trend this service has.
5. **Quarantine from other exotics unless co-housed at home** — a shared airspace is a shared infection until proven otherwise.
:::

## Daily monitoring

- Every patient is weighed at the same point in the shift each day, on the same scale.
- Water intake is estimated against the species' expected range; a silent drop over two days is a clinical finding, not a housekeeping one.
- A patient that stops thermoregulating on its own has its gradient narrowed and the clinician informed the same shift.

> [!tip] Photograph the enclosure on admission and after every change. A husbandry dispute is settled in seconds by two dated photographs, and never by memory.

## Carbon dioxide and ventilation

Enclosures are ventilated so that carbon dioxide (CO~2~), which is heavier than air and pools in a closed terrarium, never accumulates at the animal's resting height. Open a ventilation gap at the top of the enclosure, not only at the base.
