**Owner:** Emergency & Critical Care · **Review cycle:** Every 12 months · **Applies to:** Staff who request, collect, or administer blood products

## Purpose

Get compatible product into the patient without a single avoidable step, and notice a transfusion reaction while it is still reversible. Every step below is done by two people: one to act, one to check.

## Before product is released

:::steps
1. **Confirm the indication** — the attending veterinarian states the reason for transfusion and the target, and it is written in the record.
2. **Take and label the pre-transfusion sample** — the sample is labelled at the patient's side, never after walking away.
3. **Cross-match and confirm the plan** — blood bank releases product only against a completed request form and a matched sample.
4. **Prepare the giving set and a second line** — the transfusion line carries no other fluids or drugs.
5. **Collect with a second checker** — the collector and the checker read the unit's labels aloud together against the patient's record.
:::

> [!protocol] Two people, at the bedside, read the unit number, the patient's identity, and the product's expiry aloud before the line is connected. A transfusion connected by one person from memory is a protocol breach regardless of the outcome.

## Administering

- The first minutes are run slowly and the patient is watched continuously by a person whose only task is the watch.
- Vital parameters are recorded at the start, early, and at the intervals the veterinarian sets.
- The giving set is dated and changed at the interval printed on it, not at the end of the shift.

> [!critical] If a reaction is suspected — a sudden rise in temperature, a change in breathing, a new rash, or a collapse — stop the transfusion **immediately**, keep the line for sampling, and call the veterinarian. ==Never restart a unit after a suspected reaction.== The unit and the giving set go to the blood bank intact.

## The first minutes

The observation during the first minutes of a transfusion is the whole safety net, because a reaction found early is a clinical problem and one found late is a mortality review. The person watching does not leave the patient, does not answer the phone, and does not start another task.

## After the transfusion

- The outcome against the stated target is charted by the veterinarian who ordered it.
- Any reaction, however mild, is reported to the blood bank the same day, whether or not the unit was fully administered.
- The unused portion of a unit is returned to the blood bank for its own decision, never discarded by the ward.[^1]

[^1]: Returning the unit is what allows the blood bank to quarantine the donor and inspect the storage chain; discarding it destroys the only evidence of a possible storage fault.
