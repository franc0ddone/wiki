**Owner:** Medical Oncology · **Review cycle:** Every 12 months · **Applies to:** Staff who administer or assist with cytotoxic chemotherapy

## Purpose

Extravasation — cytotoxic drug leaking into tissue around the vein — is rare, time-critical, and almost always preventable. This procedure is the response plan; the prevention rules live in the oncology administration checklist and are not repeated here.

> [!protocol] Treat every extravasation as an emergency, even when the volume is small and the drug is a known irritant rather than a vesicant. The window to limit tissue damage is measured in minutes, and the attending oncologist is called **first**, not after the line is pulled.

## Recognising it

Stop the infusion the moment any of these appear, and leave the catheter in place until the oncologist and the extravasation kit arrive.

| Sign {colspan=2} | What staff notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Patient behaviour | During handling | Sudden vocalisation, flinching, or pulling the limb away |
| Local tissue | At the site | Swelling, blanching, or a change in the limb's warmth or colour |
| The port | During the draw | Resistance on the syringe, or fluid at the insertion site |

## Immediate response

:::steps
1. **Stop the infusion** — clamp the line. Do not flush it, and do not remove the catheter.
2. **Call the attending oncologist** — state the drug, the estimated volume that may have leaked, and the site.
3. **Bring the extravasation kit** — the oncologist directs which agents are applied; the kit is not opened speculatively.
4. **Elevate the limb** — where the site allows, and keep the patient calm and warm.
5. **Mark and photograph the site** — outline the area with a skin marker and attach the photograph to the record.
6. **Chart the timeline** — the moment the infusion started, the moment it stopped, and the moment the oncologist arrived.
:::

> [!critical] Never flush a suspected extravasation to "clear the line", and never massage the site. Both push more drug into tissue. If you are unsure whether the line is still patent, treat it as an extravasation until the oncologist says otherwise.

## After the acute response

- The site is assessed at each recheck and photographed under the same lighting until resolution.
- **==Every extravasation is reviewed at the next oncology huddle==**, whether or not it caused harm, so that line placement technique is corrected rather than repeated.
- The incident is logged in the patient record and the departmental safety log the same day.

## Documentation

The record names the drug, the estimated extravasated volume, the time to oncologist arrival, the agents applied, and the follow-up schedule. Vague entries ("swelling noted, oncology aware") are returned for completion by the oncologist.
