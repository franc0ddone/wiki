**Owner:** Emergency & Critical Care · **Review cycle:** Every 12 months · **Applies to:** ER clinical staff and client services

## Purpose

Give every patient arriving at the emergency door the same first five minutes, whether the waiting room is empty or twelve deep. Triage decides *how fast* a patient is seen — it never decides *whether* a client is registered, and it is never a diagnosis.

## Triage categories

Assign a category at first contact, before registration if the lobby is under pressure. A category can move in either direction at any point, and the attending veterinarian's reassessment always wins.

| Category | Presentation | Target to first assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level 1 | Immediate | Vocal, active, or deteriorating |
| Level 2 | Urgent | Stable but needs a veterinarian now |
| Level 3 | Standard | Comfortable, ambulatory, not critical |

> [!protocol] Any team member may escalate a patient to Level 1 and call the back for the treatment table. Escalating is never a disciplinary matter — a false alarm costs a few minutes, a missed one costs an animal.

## First five minutes

:::steps
1. **Assess from the doorway** — note mentation, respiratory effort, and whether the patient is ambulatory before you touch them.
2. **Ask the one question that changes the plan** — "how long, and is it getting worse?" Record the answer verbatim.
3. **Place a triage card** — patient name, presenting complaint, category, and time of arrival on the outside of the card holder only.
4. **Move the critical patient straight back** — a Level 1 goes to the treatment table; registration is completed afterwards by CSR.
5. **Return to the lobby** — log the triage in the intake sheet and call the next client forward.
:::

> [!critical] Never leave a Level 1 patient alone in the lobby to complete paperwork. If billing information is needed, take a photo of the card and complete it after the patient is on the table.

## Handoff to the treatment floor

The triage nurse hands to the receiving technician in one sentence: *"<patient>, <presenting complaint>, arrived at <time>, I am worried about <organ system>."* The receiving technician repeats the concern back. Nothing else is required — a handoff that takes forty seconds is one that happens.

## Documentation

- The intake sheet records arrival time, triage category, and every category change with a timestamp.
- A client who leaves without being seen is logged as an *against-advice departure*, not as a discharge.
- The morning huddle reviews any Level 1 whose category was set more than five minutes after arrival.
