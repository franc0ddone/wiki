**Owner:** Infection Control · **Review cycle:** Every 6 months · **Applies to:** All clinical and support staff who clean equipment

## Purpose

Cleaning and disinfection are two different jobs and neither substitutes for the other. A visible mess is removed first, then an agent is applied and given the contact time it needs. Skipping to the agent on a soiled surface disinfects the dirt, not the equipment.

## The three-step sequence

| Step {colspan=2} | What it removes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Clean | Wash, then dry | Visible organic material, which inactivates most agents on contact |
| 2. Disinfect | Apply the agent | The organisms the service has decided are unacceptable |
| 3. Dry | Store dry | The moisture that lets whatever survived keep growing |

> [!protocol] The agent's wet contact time is read from the manufacturer's label for the product in front of you, every time. A remembered number is not a contact time — formulations change, and so do the concentrations the ward mixes.

## Choosing an agent

Match the agent to the organism and the surface. When the target organism is unknown, the ward defaults to the agent the infection control lead has listed for the bay.

| Surface | Agent | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stainless and hard plastic | Accelerated hydrogen peroxide | Compatible with most equipment; check cables and cuffs first |
| Floors, drains, and runs | Chlorine-based dilution | Mixed fresh; never stored mixed |
| Delicate instruments | Manufacturer's validated method | Follow the instrument's own instructions over the ward default |
| Exotics housing | Amphibian- and invert-safe agents only | Many standard agents are toxic to these patients |

## Doing it properly

:::steps
1. **Clear the surface** — remove visible material with a cloth, and discard the cloth, do not rinse and reuse it.
2. **Clean first** — wash with detergent and water, then dry, before any disinfectant is applied.
3. **Apply the agent at the label's working concentration** — mixed, not estimated.
4. **Respect the wet contact time** — the surface stays visibly wet for the full duration; an agent that dries early has not finished its job.
5. **Rinse where the label requires it** — some agents are corrosive to instruments or patients if left to dry.
6. **Dry and store** — wet equipment stored in a cupboard grows what the last two steps removed.
:::

> [!warning] Chlorine agents are mixed fresh and discarded at the end of the task. A bottle of "bleach solution" of unknown age is treated as water and thrown out rather than used.

## Documentation and audit

- Each bay logs its cleaning at the end of every occupied stay, and terminal cleans are signed.
- The infection control lead audits a random sample of bays weekly, checking contact-time compliance rather than cleanliness alone.
- Repeated audits that find equipment stored wet are escalated to the service lead, not just re-cleaned.
