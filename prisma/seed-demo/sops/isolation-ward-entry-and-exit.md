**Owner:** Infectious Disease Control Committee · **Review cycle:** Every 6 months · **Applies to:** All staff entering the isolation ward

## Purpose

Keep organisms inside the ward and staff outside the risk. This procedure covers the doorway itself — donning, doffing, and the movement of equipment across the barrier — and is deliberately separate from any disease-specific isolation protocol.

## Barrier standards

Isolation bays are single-occupancy wherever the ward allows it. Cohorting a second patient in an occupied bay is permitted only with the attending veterinarian's written approval in the ward log, and never across two different suspected organisms.

| Zone | What belongs there | What may never enter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clean side | Fresh PPE, ward log, wall-mounted thermometer | Anything that has been inside a bay |
| Barrier | Dedicated gowns, shoe covers, hand hygiene station | Phones, personal bags, exposed wrists |
| Dirty side | Dedicated mop head, waste, dirty laundry hamper | Reusable equipment leaving the bay |

## Donning

:::steps
1. **Confirm the patient's isolation status** — read the cage card, not the whiteboard, which lags behind.
2. **Perform hand hygiene** — the ward's alcohol rub is effective against enveloped organisms only; wash with soap when in doubt.
3. **Gown, then gloves** — glove cuffs overlap the gown sleeve, never the other way around.
4. **Add shoe covers and eye protection** — eye protection is mandatory for any task that could splash.
5. **Enter with nothing you cannot leave behind** — stethoscopes and thermometers stay in the bay for the duration.
:::

> [!warning] A gown is single-use per patient. Changing bays without regowning is the single most common break in the ward's barrier, and it is the one the weekly audit is most likely to catch.

## Exiting and doffing

- Remove gloves first, then gown, then eye protection, then shoe covers — each into the correct bin, each after hand hygiene.
- Hand hygiene is performed twice at the doorway: once with gloves on, once bare-handed.
- A patient is not cleared from isolation until the disease-specific protocol's clearance criteria are met; a negative screening test alone is never sufficient.

> [!critical] Reusable equipment, however clean it looks, does not cross the barrier. Equipment that must leave is disinfected inside the bay and transferred clean-side only after the disinfectant's full contact time has elapsed.[^1]

## Documentation

Every entry and exit is timestamped in the ward log with the visitor's role and the patient's bay number. The log is the record of who was exposed when a positive culture comes back, so it is completed in the moment, not reconstructed later.[^2]

[^1]: Contact times are printed on the manufacturer's label for the specific product; use the label, not a remembered number.
[^2]: A log completed at the end of the shift is treated as a protocol deviation by the safety audit.
